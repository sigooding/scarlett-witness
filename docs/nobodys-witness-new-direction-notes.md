# NOBODY'S WITNESS (New Direction) — editorial notes on Scenes 1–2

The brand-new story (`src/data/nobodysWitnessNew.ts`, id `nobodys-witness-new`),
registered first in the catalogue. The other Nobody's Witness
(`nobodysWitness.ts` — "Nobody's Witness (2026 Draft)") stays in the catalogue
untouched, as instructed. Scenes 1–2 only, True Romance style, Loyal,
Nebraska, October 1994. Part One ends on a fade to black — no ending yet —
with a Part Two note node on the canvas waiting for Scene 3.

## Verdict

The strongest script in the project so far. Scene 2 is essentially all
dialogue and it holds: the banter has genuine True Romance rhythm (fast,
warm, funny, one honest line hiding in the jokes) and the closing beat —
"One truck. Nobody in it." — is creepier than the Redux's knock because
nothing *happens*. And the title finally fits the material: the whole piece
is about being witnessed, and the line that says it arrives mid-scene,
unannounced — "I just notice things, it's a whole personality flaw of mine."
That's the thesis of Nobody's Witness and it lands as a shrug.

## What's working (keep verbatim)

- **The opening question.** "You want coffee or you want to tell me why
  you're here?" Character established in one line — direct, practical,
  warm — and it sets up the scene's engine: she wants to know, he won't
  say, and she stops asking and tells him what she knows instead.
- **The surveillance monologue replaces the recognition beat.** The Redux
  hinge was Vera *recognizing* Jack; here the whole town has been watching
  since Thursday — Denny at the Sinclair, the two-and-a-half-mile walk, the
  red ears. More specific, funnier, more threatening, and it defers the
  name reveal so her competence does the work. "Small towns, honey,
  everybody's your own personal FBI whether they mean to be or not." —
  tagline-grade.
- **"Bullshit."** The engine of the scene. Jack hides; she calls it flat,
  with zero heat. Her power is making honesty feel like the cheapest
  option. And the wet coat is the best image in the piece: "a story sitting
  on you like a wet coat, honey, I can see the shape of it from here. You
  just don't want to hang it up yet."
- **The pivot instead of the push.** "Fine. You don't wanna talk about you,
  let's talk about what you missed." — giving the out is how you get the
  truth, and the catch-up (the wrong-blue water tower, watching somebody
  else's fireworks from a ditch off Highway 4) is the best world-building in
  the franchise while carrying the theme: three years of nothing.
  "One woman cried." is perfect.
- **The ending.** A static frame, not an event. The knock was a threat with
  a door; the truck is a threat with no door. "No ending yet" is the right
  instinct — a cliff, not a cliffhanger button.
- **Vera's backstory arrives as jokes.** "Cancer, the fast kind… that's the
  mercy version, though nobody tells you that part till after," and "I annoy
  him about it daily, which is basically my whole parenting strategy" — the
  mother/Cody material is carried by comedy, the True Romance trick. She's a
  person now, not a warm spotlight.
- **The four Daryls.** Grief buried in a joke ("Daryl's grandmother died.
  Not the pie one. Different Daryl.") is pulp-serial planting in disguise.
  Keep it exactly as written.

## Suggested improvements, in priority order

### 1. Trim the pie-is-breakfast set by a third
Six beats is one beat too long for a middle-of-scene comic set. Keepers:
"a dessert wearing a little fruit costume" and "smoked two packs a day and
outlived three husbands, Daryl, she wasn't operating on the same rules as
the rest of us." "Fruit's breakfast. That's just basic nutrition." and
"Lived to ninety-one." are cuttable — the third beat of a comic set is
where reels lose tempo. (The reel keeps all of it for fidelity; if the
script trims, delete beats `nwn_s_nutrition` and `nwn_s_grandma`.)

### 2. Close the loop on "why you're here"
The scene opens on the question and ends without the answer. Two options:
- **(a)** Let the last V.O. carry it — "Five days out. I never did tell her
  why I was here. She stopped asking after the second cup, which is when I
  knew she already had an answer." Her *not* asking becomes the tell.
- **(b)** One Vera line under "whether you're gonna turn out to be an
  asshole": "And for the record, I don't think the bus is why you're here
  either."

### 3. The motel room needs one prop
Scene 1 is three beats of a man doing nothing — which is the point — but a
reel needs a hook across the four-day gap. Keep "he doesn't turn on the
TV" and add a hold-to-EXAMINE hotspot on the STATE PROPERTY bag:
*"Stenciled. Your initials aren't on it. You don't check."* Converts the
gap into something the player does and plants the bag as a Part Two prop.
(The reel leaves the bag in the room prose; the hotspot is a one-line add in
Studio whenever you want it.)

### 4. One word: "walk the lot twice" → "walk Weller's lot twice"
As written, "the lot" is ambiguous (the Sinclair's? Weller's?). One
possessive fixes a line that otherwise reads like a mistake.

### 5. Plant the parole check-in
The old versions had the Friday county-office beat (the PO writes down
where he's staying); the new script dropped it, and a parolee starting
work is a detail a reader who knows will catch. One V.O. sentence in the
room scene does triple duty (timeline + parole logic + why Weller):
"Friday I drove to the county office and a man I'd never met wrote down
where I was staying. Monday I start at Weller's, because Weller takes cons
— cons work cheap and don't file complaints."

### 6. Let Daryl react to his own joke
After "There's four Daryls" and Vera's laugh at her own joke, one stage
direction: the pie Daryl doesn't look up. Doesn't stop eating either. That
is the town's rhythm — grief and pie coexisting. One narrator beat.

### 7. "I don't actually care what you did" — she doesn't know what he did
She's extending grace to a crime she hasn't named. Fine as character
(generosity as strategy) and it's the scene's emotional turn, so keep it —
but "I don't actually care what it *was*" costs nothing and removes the
presumption. Optional.

### 8. Math notes (all defensible — just be deliberate)
Thursday arrival → Monday diner: "five days out" works if you count both
days as full (Thu–Mon); "four days to get over that habit" = Fri–Mon;
Vera was 22 when her mama died, Cody 9→16 — all seven-year gaps. The math
holds. State the five-days count once on purpose and never question it again.

### 9. The town is Loyal, the diner is Loyal, and the tower says LOYAL in
the wrong blue
That's accidental theme — loyalty without the exact shade. Keep it and use
it: Vera should say the word "loyal" once in Part Two, at the moment the
real loyalty question opens.
