# THE LATE SHIFT (REDUX) — editorial notes on the revised Part One script

The brand-new version (`src/data/theLateShiftRedux.ts`, id `the-late-shift-redux`).
The earlier reimagined version stays in the catalogue untouched (`theLateShiftReimagined.ts`).

## What the rewrite fixed (it's working)

- **Part One now has its pulp incident.** The original Scene 2 ended on warmth
  with a vague wrong note; this one ends on a *machine*: a car that circles the
  lot twice, Vera killing the booth lights, a two-knuckle knock, and a V.O.
  that turns the whole scene retroactively sinister ("And somebody already knew
  where to find me."). That's a cliffhanger with a button on it.
- **Vera's recognition beat is now engineered ambiguity, not authorial
  undecidedness.** "Not surprise. Recognition, and then a decision. Then the
  warmth comes down over it like a shade." That single stage direction is the
  hinge the whole serial swings on — it commits to Vera knowing *something*,
  without telling us what. Keep it exactly as written.
- **The robbery contradiction is now intentional, and Vera catches it.**
  "Robbing part was you, but you were just there. That's a lot of man for one
  sentence, honey." Then she lets it go — and "the mercy — she lets it go" is
  the best mercy beat in the script because we can't tell if it's kindness or
  patience. It's both, and the reader can't be sure which one matters.
- **The house choice is now physical.** A/B/C aren't "look or don't look" —
  they're three different ways of standing in the road, and "a curtain moves in
  the front window — or doesn't" is the strongest of them because it makes the
  *absence* do work.
- **Vera's dialogue is doing comedy and threat at the same time** ("Third pie
  this week, Billy… I'm going to start charging you rent on that booth"). The
  register stays warm so the cold notes (Weller's. *Not a bit.*) land cold.
- **"Some doors you don't knock on twice"** — planted in Scene 1, and Scene 2
  ends on someone *knocking*. The theme and the plot are now the same sentence.

## Suggested improvements for the next pass

### 1. Earn "Cody still around?" one micro-tell earlier
Vera knowing Jack's brother's name is the best hook in the piece — but right
now it's also the only impossible fact in an otherwise grounded scene. Plant
one small thing so the reader goes *"oh, that's how she knew"* instead of
*"wait, how does she know?"* Cheapest fix: in the recognition beat, she glances
at the PROPERTY OF STATE bag — stenciled "MORROW, J." — and that's the "fast,
small, gone" thing in her face. She read the name and she remembers the
Morrows. Then "Cody still around?" becomes a *test*, not a miracle. (Flag for
Part Two: Vera never lets anything go — she just files it. The mercy is real,
and it's also a ledger.)

### 2. The Weller's beat can carry more weight
"Weller's." + "something crosses her face — quick" + "He pays Fridays. Cash,
mostly." is good, but it's the one place the script tells instead of showing.
Suggested addition, one line: Vera's hand pauses on a cup she's been drying —
then she sets it down and the warmth comes back up like a shade. You already
have the "shade" image from the recognition beat; repeating it *on the same
name* tells the reader these two beats rhyme, and that Weller is the man she
knew he'd be working for before he said it. That's your "Vera is waiting for
something" tell.

### 3. One V.O. can be deleted
"You had one job, one sandwich, and one conversation" is a keeper — it does
the counting that makes the knock land. But "Some doors you don't knock on
twice" repeats verbatim across all three house branches. In a reel the player
has just *made* the choice, so they only hear it once — fine — but consider
cutting it from the A branch (he didn't engage with the house at all; the line
fits B and C better) and let the silent walk be its own verdict.

### 4. Timeline: make the four days audible
He arrives Thursday morning; "Friday I drove out to Beatrice" (parole
check-in — good detail, keep it); Monday he starts at Weller's. The script
counts "four days" correctly, but only in a V.O. the player may skim. One
visible beat would fix it: the motel room has a calendar or a newspaper with
Monday's date already circled — a small hotspot/examine in the reel, or one
narrator line ("Thursday to Monday is four days when you're counting them.").

### 5. Vera is one flaw short of pulp-proof
She's funny, kind, perceptive, unfazed by prison, philosophically wise — the
original critique's point #3 still applies to the rewrite. The "Cody" line is
the natural place to let one small human crack show *through* the charm:
e.g. after Jack answers "…Yeah.", Vera's count of the till goes quiet a half
beat too long before "Mm." — as if the name costs her something too. That way
she's not just omniscient; she has her own unfinished business with the
Morrows, and the reader starts asking whose side that puts her on.

### 6. Scene 3 must pay the knock immediately
Pulp serials never open the next installment on the answer. Suggested open:
the door opens *onto* Weller — he already knew Jack was in the diner because
he gave him the job, and "I was in the neighborhood" is the lie he tells while
Vera watches. The robbery's "somebody else" is the man who owns this town's
only garage, and Cody is the name that connects them. Whatever you choose,
Part Two's first line of dialogue should be whoever's on the other side of
that glass — don't cut away to the next morning.

### 7. Small line notes
- "He pays Fridays. Cash, mostly. You'll want to know that." — the best
  sentence in the Weller's exchange. Consider Vera not looking at Jack when
  she says it.
- The "survival story" meatloaf line is the perfect cold open for Vera; the
  redux wisely keeps it. Don't let any later version explain it.
- "That's a lot of man for one sentence, honey." — keep verbatim. It does in
  one line what a paragraph of backstory couldn't.
- The knocked-in beat ("Two knuckles. Unhurried.") — keep the *same* phrasing
  for the second knock ("Same two knuckles. Same patience."). Repetition is
  the threat. It's already written that way — do not vary it.

  him (add a passing-through/evasive branch later if you want a second path).

## Bottom line

The revised Part One is the right version. It fixes the two structural
complaints about the reimagined draft — no incident (now: the knock) and
unengineered Vera knowledge (now: the shade beat) — and the new ending is a
proper pulp cliffhanger. The remaining work is texture: one tell for how Vera
knows about Cody, one shared image between the Weller's beat and the
recognition beat, and Scene 3 opening on the man at the door.
